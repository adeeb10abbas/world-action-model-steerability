# Reviewer assessment and editorial revision

Reviewed September 27, 2026. This is an internal reviewer assessment of the current manuscript and recorded evidence, not an external review or an award prediction.

## Overall judgment

I lean toward accepting this as a focused empirical workshop paper, with moderate confidence. It has a clear controlled question, matched physical starts, a visible relation-specific effect, and an instructive mismatch between relational scoring and the requested manipulation. I would not nominate the current evidence for an award yet. Its novelty and diagnostic reach remain narrower than its potential framing: it demonstrates behavioral sensitivity, but does not locate the failure inside language interpretation, prediction, planning, or control.

The strongest contribution is the conjunction of two observations. Equivalent relational descriptions can change behavior even when the desired arrangement and starting state are fixed. Meanwhile, the arrangement being true is not sufficient evidence that the requested manipulation occurred. Together, these motivate evaluating whether the intended manipulation is preserved across descriptions, rather than treating a spatial score as a complete test of instruction following.

## Strengths

- Shared physical starts and matched first observations make scene difficulty a controlled factor in the wording comparison. Different trajectories can still introduce different execution difficulty; the experiment does not isolate an internal language module.
- The paper reports relation-level heterogeneity, including a counterexample to the pooled direction, instead of presenting the average as a universal language deficit.
- The distinction between first stable attainment and the final state is useful when the continued-execution stopping rule is explicit.
- The manuscript acknowledges the original study scope and the unresolved prediction analysis. That disclosure supports the credibility of the narrower behavioral claim.

## Main weaknesses and what addresses them

1. **The argument initially stopped at paraphrase sensitivity.** A workshop reader needs a clearer reason to care. The revised discussion now connects goal descriptions, intended manipulation, and scored outcomes. This improves communication; it does not add empirical novelty by itself.
2. **The endpoint is easier to satisfy than the full instruction.** A reference can move, and initially satisfied goals can pass before manipulation. The revised Methods states this directly. The paper should not relabel relation passes as instruction-following successes or retrospectively discard inconvenient passes.
3. **The main figures summarize different populations.** The paired contrast includes initially satisfied conditions, all of which contribute zero differences for stable-ever. The heatmap displays achievement conditions separately. This is now explicit. The zero-difference statement does not apply to terminal outcomes.
4. **The headline effect is concentrated, and the bowl condition has a semantic mismatch.** The manuscript now notes a descriptive sensitivity excluding bowl stacking. This is not a new confirmatory significance claim. More linguistic forms would be needed to separate relation vocabulary from argument order.
5. **Motion summaries are not a complete behavioral explanation.** No detected lift is not equivalent to no manipulation; lateral goals can be achieved by pushing. Full paired videos are still needed before describing a specific action sequence or mechanism. The current paper uses cautious descriptions of the recorded fields.
6. **Connection to world models is architectural context, not demonstrated causal necessity.** This study does not compare a matched action-only system or establish whether predictions anticipate execution failures. The revised discussion treats this as a diagnostic question for future work, not a finding.

## Changes made in this revision

- Replaced the workshop-focused discussion and repeated setup inventory with three connected paragraphs about preserving intended manipulation across descriptions, interpreting heterogeneous score changes, and diagnosing prediction-versus-execution failure.
- Clarified that reference-first refers to the subject of the spatial clause, not a camera viewpoint change.
- Used “converse relation” so the intervention does not sound like a change of physical goal.
- Distinguished retention under continued control from a deployment that stops on success.
- Removed an abstract implication that recorded summaries establish wrong-object manipulation in every relevant episode.
- Moved the study-scope disclosure into Methods, retaining the post-outcome change of paper focus and annotation amendment.
- Kept numerical results, figures, and raw outcomes unchanged. Added one descriptive exclusion check in prose from existing saved analysis.

## Existing-data checks behind the added clarification

The exclusion sensitivity is derived from `analysis/paper_results.json`, using `paired_state_effects` with `model == pooled`, `contrast == S-I`, and `scene != S5`. The remaining 24 equally weighted physical-state differences average to 0.1064815 (10.65 percentage points). No new confidence interval or significance claim is made for this exclusion.

The same artifact records `outcomes.pooled.maintenance.all.stable_ever = 216/216`. A check against the frozen state registry yields 72 complete D/S/I triplets, all passing stable-ever in every wording, hence zero paired differences. Terminal outcomes are different and are not covered by this statement.

## What would most improve award competitiveness

The highest-value next step is to inspect the four already specified recordings in `VIDEO_REQUEST.md` and present the two matched pairs clearly: one showing the wording-dependent outcome gap, and one showing why a relation pass can be misleading. These are illustrative, outcome-selected examples, not additional statistical evidence.

The next empirical improvement would be a prospectively specified, role-aware evaluation and a small factorized wording replication on the strongest task pair. That would test the proposed explanation more directly than adding many loosely connected tasks. Do not launch these experiments without agreeing on the question and endpoint first.

A polished discussion makes the current contribution easier to assess. An award case would require the central behavioral explanation to survive those stronger checks; neither the present evidence nor a prose revision supports a probability of winning.
